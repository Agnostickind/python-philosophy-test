What is a School of Philosophy?

A philosophical school is a tradition of thought built around a shared approach to some of the most fundamental questions of human existence. Schools of philosophy may differ in their beliefs about reality, knowledge, ethics, human nature, society, and the meaning of a good life, yet each develops its own methods and arguments for understanding these questions. Some emerged around the teachings of a particular philosopher, while others developed gradually through generations of thinkers who expanded, challenged, or reinterpreted earlier ideas.

Throughout history, people have searched for answers to life's greatest questions. Rather than reaching the same conclusions, philosophers developed different ways of understanding reality, knowledge, morality, and human purpose. These distinct approaches gradually formed what we now call schools of philosophy—traditions of thought that share common principles while offering unique perspectives on the world.

Each school reflects a different way of thinking. Some encourage careful reasoning and logical analysis, while others emphasize virtue, inner peace, experience, or spiritual wisdom. Together, these schools demonstrate that philosophy is not a single set of beliefs but a rich collection of ideas that has evolved across cultures and centuries. Studying them allows us to compare different viewpoints, appreciate intellectual diversity, and develop a deeper understanding of human thought.

From the schools of Ancient Greece and the diverse traditions of Indian philosophy to Chinese and Japanese thought, Islamic and Persian philosophy, the intellectual movements of the Enlightenment, and the many modern philosophical schools, these traditions represent different attempts to make sense of the world and our place within it. Exploring them side by side reveals not only their profound differences, but also recurring questions about truth, morality, freedom, knowledge, existence, and human flourishing.

🏛 Ancient Schools

Stoicism

Founder: Zeno of Citium

Focus on what you can control. Virtue is the highest good.

  • Seneca
  • Epictetus
  • Marcus Aurelius

Epicureanism

Founder: Epicurus

Happiness comes from simple pleasures, friendship and freedom from fear.

  • Epicurus

Cynicism

Founder: Antisthenes

Reject luxury and live according to nature.

  • Diogenes

Skepticism

Founder: Pyrrho

Question everything. Suspend judgement until evidence appears.

Peripateticism

Founder: Aristotle

Study nature, logic and ethics through careful observation and reasoned inquiry into the world around us.

  • Aristotle
  • Theophrastus

Neoplatonism

Founder: Plotinus

All of reality flows from a single, transcendent source called "The One," and the soul seeks to return to it.

  • Plotinus
  • Porphyry

Atomism

Founder: Leucippus & Democritus

Everything that exists is made of indivisible atoms moving through empty void. Nothing exists but atoms and space.

  • Leucippus
  • Democritus

Sophism

Associated with: Protagoras

Truth and morality are relative to the individual or the city. Mastery of persuasion and rhetoric matters more than absolute knowledge.

  • Protagoras
  • Gorgias

🕉 Indian Schools

Buddhism

Founder: Gautama Buddha

Life contains suffering. Freedom comes through wisdom and compassion.

Vedanta

Reality is one. The individual soul and ultimate reality are deeply connected.

Jainism

Founder: Mahavira

Practice strict non-violence and self-discipline to purify the soul and reach liberation.

  • Mahavira

Zen Buddhism

Direct experience and meditation matter more than scripture or ritual in reaching enlightenment.

  • Bodhidharma
  • Dōgen

Samkhya

Founder: Kapila

One of the oldest Indian schools, explaining reality through two principles: Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter).

  • Kapila

Yoga

Founder: Patanjali

A practical path of meditation, ethical discipline and physical practice to still the mind and reach liberation.

  • Patanjali

Nyaya

Founder: Akshapada Gautama

Focuses on logic, reasoning and the valid means by which true knowledge can be gained.

  • Akshapada Gautama

Vaisheshika

Founder: Kanada

An early Indian atomist school, explaining reality through distinct, irreducible categories and physical atoms.

  • Kanada

Mimamsa

Founder: Jaimini

Centers on the correct interpretation of Vedic ritual and the nature of dharma (duty).

  • Jaimini

Charvaka (Lokayata)

Founder: Brihaspati (traditional)

A materialist and skeptical school that rejected the afterlife and accepted only direct perception as valid knowledge.

☯ Chinese & ⛩ Japanese

Confucianism

Founder: Confucius

Build a just society through morality, education and respect.

  • Confucius
  • Mencius

Taoism

Founder: Lao Tzu

Live in harmony with nature and follow the Tao.

  • Lao Tzu
  • Zhuangzi

Legalism

Founder: Han Feizi

A strong state relies on strict laws, clear rewards and punishments, rather than moral virtue alone.

  • Han Feizi
  • Shang Yang

Mohism

Founder: Mozi

Advocates universal love, equality and practical ethics aimed at benefiting society as a whole.

  • Mozi

Neo-Confucianism

A later revival of Confucian ethics blended with metaphysical ideas absorbed from Buddhism and Taoism.

  • Zhu Xi
  • Wang Yangming

Kyoto School

Founder: Nishida Kitarō

Blends Zen Buddhist thought with Western philosophy, exploring pure experience and the nature of nothingness.

  • Nishida Kitarō

Bushidō

The way of the warrior. An ethical code of honor, loyalty, discipline and self-sacrifice central to samurai culture.

Japanese Zen

Founder: Dōgen

Zen as it developed in Japan, centered on seated meditation (zazen) as the direct path to enlightenment.

  • Dōgen
  • Eisai

🌙 Islamic & Persian Schools

Avicennism

Founder: Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

Synthesized Aristotelian philosophy with Islamic theology, building a comprehensive system of metaphysics and medicine.

  • Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

Averroism

Founder: Ibn Rushd (Averroes)

Defended reason and Aristotelian philosophy as fully compatible with faith, later shaping medieval European thought.

  • Ibn Rushd (Averroes)

Illuminationism

Founder: Suhrawardi

Combines philosophy and mysticism, treating knowledge as a form of illumination or light rather than pure logical deduction.

  • Suhrawardi

Sufism

A mystical Islamic tradition centered on the direct, personal experience of the divine through spiritual practice.

  • Rumi
  • Al-Ghazali

Kalam

Islamic scholastic theology that uses rational argumentation to defend and explain religious doctrine.

  • Al-Ghazali
  • Al-Ash'ari

Transcendent Theosophy

Founder: Mulla Sadra

A 17th-century metaphysical system blending philosophy, theology and mysticism into a single unified vision of reality.

  • Mulla Sadra

💡 Enlightenment Schools

Rationalism

Founder: René Descartes

Reason is the primary source of knowledge.

  • Descartes
  • Spinoza
  • Leibniz

Empiricism

Founder: John Locke

Knowledge comes from experience and observation.

  • Locke
  • Berkeley
  • Hume

Idealism

Founder: Plato

Reality is fundamentally mental or spiritual.

  • Plato
  • Kant
  • Hegel

Social Contract Theory

Founder: Thomas Hobbes

Political legitimacy comes from an implicit agreement among individuals to form a just and orderly society.

  • Hobbes
  • Locke
  • Rousseau

Materialism

Founder: Democritus

Everything that exists, including the mind, can ultimately be explained by physical matter.

  • Thomas Hobbes
  • Julien de La Mettrie

Deism

God created the universe but does not intervene in it, leaving it to run by natural law.

  • Voltaire
  • Thomas Paine

Natural Law Theory

Founder: Hugo Grotius

Moral principles are built into nature itself and can be discovered through reason, independent of religious authority.

  • Hugo Grotius
  • Samuel von Pufendorf

Pantheism

Founder: Baruch Spinoza

God and the universe are one and the same — there is no separation between the divine and the natural world.

  • Baruch Spinoza

Classical Liberalism

Founder: John Locke

A political philosophy emphasizing individual liberty, free markets, and strictly limited government.

  • Adam Smith
  • John Locke

Deontology

Founder: Immanuel Kant

Morality is based on following universal duties and rules, not on the consequences an action produces.

  • Immanuel Kant

🌌 Modern Schools

Existentialism

Founder: Søren Kierkegaard

Life has no predetermined meaning. You create your own purpose.

  • Sartre
  • Kierkegaard
  • Camus

Nihilism

Questions whether objective meaning or morality truly exists.

  • Nietzsche (often associated)
  • Ivan Turgenev

Pragmatism

Founder: Charles Sanders Peirce

Ideas are judged by their practical results.

  • William James
  • John Dewey

Utilitarianism

Founder:Jeremy Bentham

Choose actions that create the greatest happiness for the greatest number.

  • Jeremy Bentham
  • John Stuart Mill

Phenomenology

Founder:Edmund Husserl

Study conscious experience from the first-person point of view, focusing on how things appear to us.

  • Edmund Husserl
  • Maurice Merleau-Ponty

Analytic Philosophy

Founder:Gottlob Frege

Use logic and precise language to clarify philosophical problems and analyze meaning.

  • Bertrand Russell
  • Ludwig Wittgenstein

Marxism

Founder: Karl Marx

Society is shaped by class struggle and economic systems, and history moves through material conditions.

  • Karl Marx
  • Friedrich Engels

Absurdism

Founder:Albert Camus

Life has no inherent meaning, yet humans can still live with dignity, freedom and purpose despite this.

  • Albert Camus

Structuralism

Founder:Ferdinand de Saussure

Human culture, language and thought are best understood through the underlying structures and systems beneath them.

  • Ferdinand de Saussure
  • Claude Lévi-Strauss

Postmodernism

Challenges grand narratives and absolute truths, arguing that meaning is shaped by language, power and context.

  • Michel Foucault
  • Jacques Derrida

Logical Positivism

Founder:Moritz Schlick

A statement is only meaningful if it can be verified through logic or empirical observation.

  • A. J. Ayer
  • Rudolf Carnap

Critical Theory

Founder:Max Horkheimer

Examines how society, culture and ideology can dominate or liberate people, aiming toward human emancipation.

  • Theodor Adorno
  • Max Horkheimer
